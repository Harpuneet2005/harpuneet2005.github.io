SpectraSip

Near-infrared sensing for safer drinks

Interactive Intelligent Systems Laboratory

Tokyo, Japan

I’m developing a low-cost smart coaster that uses near-infrared spectroscopy to identify spectral fingerprints of adulterated drinks. To understand how the system performs beyond a controlled setup, I ran 180+ trials across drug concentrations, drink compositions, and lighting conditions, then trained a multiband spectral classifier that achieved 92% adulterant detection accuracy.

Taking the prototype into Tokyo nightlife venues made ambient light a central engineering challenge. Across four hardware iterations, I reduced that interference by 75%, combining optical sensing, classification, and practical hardware design.

02 Sensing Mar 2025 — Apr 2026

Opto-Dye

Dye-free imaging for forensic science

Wheeler Microfluidics Laboratory

Toronto, Canada

With Prof. Aaron Wheeler and the Ontario Centre for Forensic Sciences, I developed a microfluidic–optical system to make forensic imaging more accessible. A custom multispectral LED array and a standard microscope supported dye-free cell identification across 350–1080 nm. Selected as one of 25 Laidlaw Research Scholars, I received $12,500 in funding to pursue the work.

I used Detectron2 and μSAM to classify unstained, multichannel microscopy samples and integrated the imaging platform with a microfluidic DNA-analysis chip, reducing sample processing time by 70%. Validation on 200+ samples achieved 97% accuracy against traditional staining and hyperspectral methods. The resulting portable, low-power imaging unit was 50 times cheaper than comparable commercial systems.

Microfluidic integration Cell segmentation Multispectral microscopy

03 Sensing Apr 2024 — Dec 2025

ChromaFlash

Making material differences visible

Computational Health & Interaction Laboratory

Toronto, Canada

Working with Prof. Alex Mariakakis, I explored how a programmable LED flash could make material differences easier to see with everyday cameras. I characterized 50+ materials across 400–900 nm and developed a gradient-based optimization algorithm to select illumination patterns that maximize spectral separability.

To turn those patterns into precise illumination, I designed high-speed PCBs in Altium with 7 ns LED switching and implemented per-channel brightness and timing control on a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 using C++ and MicroPython. The system produced 13 dB material contrast, 10 nm illumination granularity, and 800 lumens of scene brightness.

High-speed PCB design Illumination optimization Spectral characterization

AgroArmor

Modular biogas for rural agriculture

United Nations Innovation Laboratory

Rome, Italy

I designed a scalable, modular biogas plant for rural agricultural settings, focusing on a system that could be maintained and repaired affordably. Using Altium, I engineered custom PCBs and IoT sensor modules for low-power slurry agitation, automated gas-flow control, and real-time monitoring.

I prototyped the hardware in Fusion 360 and AutoCAD and modeled gas flow and power consumption in MATLAB. Integrating eco-friendly materials reduced costs by 30% while supporting durability and repairability. The project won first place in the UN Transformative Research Challenge and secured $5,000 in research funding.

Mersivity / SWIM

Accessible, brain-controlled immersive technology

Mannlabs

Toronto, Canada

Under Prof. Steve Mann, I worked on inclusive immersive technology for people with spinal muscular atrophy. I built three Sequential Wave Imaging devices that create a VR environment from a single LED strip, using a custom EasyEDA PCB and an ESP32 Feather to control addressable LEDs.

To connect the visual experience to a direct brain interface, I integrated a Muse InteraXon EEG headband. Using Python and C++, I translated sensor readings into VR actions and visible waveforms, bringing together biosignal input, embedded control, and accessible interaction.

EEG integration Sequential Wave Imaging Embedded LED control

Making hands-on STEM accessible

Founder · Accessible education initiative

Accessible hardware was only part of the work. I led C++ and MicroPython workshops for 150+ visually impaired students, trained 16 faculty members, and developed audio and Braille training modules so students could explore the kits independently.
